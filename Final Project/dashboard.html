PART 1: Background & Purpose

Background & Industry Context

In international maritime shipping and energy logistics management, transit security along trade lanes is critical. Approximately 80% of global trade travels via maritime shipping, requiring vessels to cross narrow geographical choke points such as the Strait of Hormuz, the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the Bosporus, and the Black Sea.

When war or territorial conflicts erupt, commercial shipping fleets face direct physical threats from missile strikes, drone attacks, and naval mines. Monitoring these hazards locally is impossible, and conventional AIS vessel transponders are often disabled for safety.

  • OSINT Objective: Establish a remote, autonomous satellite threat assessment pipeline to verify active port/military strikes near shipping routes.
  • Actionable Value: Empowers cargo planners to dynamically redirect carrier fleets, preventing catastrophic asset damage or scheduling delays.

Implications for a Shipping Company

Operating a modern fleet requires real-time routing decisions to avoid localized conflict zones and minimize fuel/demurrage expenses:

  • Dynamic Fleet Rerouting: Spikes in verified anomalies near Yemen trigger automatic detours around the Cape of Good Hope, bypassing dangerous Red Sea crossings.
  • Energy Price Hedging: Spatiotemporal threat alerts in the Persian Gulf predict global crude oil and LNG spot surges, allowing fuel schedulers to pre-hedge fuel budgets using derivatives.
  • False Alarm Prevention: By classifying natural or industrial flaring anomalies correctly, operators avoid unnecessary, highly expensive ship detours.
PART 2: Data Sources & Collection

Multidimensional Data Layers

Our data pipeline aggregates three separate levels of information to achieve robust, cross-verified threat monitoring:

  • NASA FIRMS Active Thermal Observations: Fetches active thermal anomalies from MODIS and VIIRS satellite sensors in near real-time via API endpoints.
  • GADM v4.1 Geographical Polygons: Integrates spatial boundaries at `ADM_ADM_0` (country borders) and `ADM_ADM_1` (provincial/regional divisions) layers.
  • OSINT Conflict News Pipelines: Aggregates conflict headlines, snippets, publication times, and URLs from the NYT API, Guardian API, Google News RSS, and scrapers targeting The War Zone.

Docker Database & Persistence

To ensure enterprise data integrity, all collected and processed data layers are persisted to a PostgreSQL database hosted inside a Docker container:

  • Containerized Storage: A dedicated PostgreSQL v16 Docker container (`ce49x-postgres`) running locally on port 5432.
  • Required Tables: Persists raw cleaned points to `firms_detections`, indexed headlines to `news_articles`, DBSCAN groups to `thermal_events`, and classification results to `event_matches`.
PART 3: Spatial-Temporal Analysis, Cleaning & Clustering

Data Cleaning & Noise Filtering

Before analysis, satellite observations and news articles undergo strict quality filters:

  • Satellite Observation Filters: MODIS points are kept only if confidence is > 75% and Fire Radiative Power (FRP) is > 20 MW. VIIRS low-confidence pixels (!= "L") are pruned, keeping FRP > 5 MW.
  • Spatio-Temporal Clipping: Filtered coordinates are clipped inside GADM country polygons and labeled with regional names, dropping observations occurring outside the transit borders.
  • News Content Pruning: Ingested headlines are parsed to remove timezone discrepancies, filtered using strict conflict keyword triggers (e.g. missile, drone strike, explosion), and standardized into structured CSV schemas.

DBSCAN / Union-Find Clustering

Active fire points are grouped into distinct physical thermal events using DBSCAN clustering, establishing centroids and bounds:

  • Temporal Window: Observations occurring within 6 hours of each other are joined.
  • Sensor Distance Thresholds: Integrates MODIS points within a 2,000 meter radius, and high-resolution VIIRS points within an 800 meter radius.
  • Outlier Pruning: Isolated singletons that do not form continuous temporal runs are pruned, isolating significant industrial impacts.

Satellite Sensor Calibration Differences

This polar area chart illustrates the average Fire Radiative Power (FRP) registered per sensor. Sensor characteristics must be taken into account during DBSCAN clustering parameters.

  • MODIS: registers much higher average FRP values (62.4 MW) due to its larger pixel size (1km), capturing larger cumulative energy.

PART 4: Correlation, Scoring & Classification

Correlation Matching & Multi-Dimensional Scoring

To link active fires with media-reported conflict events, we implement strict matching constraints and a robust scoring model:

  • Spatial Matching Must: The cluster centroid and the news article's location mention **must** share the same GADM country boundary.
  • Temporal Matching Must: The publication date of the news article **must** fall within a **temporal window** of -24 to +72 hours of the cluster's earliest active detection.
  • Multi-Dimensional Scoring Model: the `total_score` combines:
    • *Time Proximity Score* (exponential decay of the time delta)
    • *Semantic Action Score* (weighting keywords like missile, drone strike, explosion)
    • *Fire/Utility Indicator Score* (weighting keywords like blackout, grid, burning)

Logistic Regression & Decision Trees

To classify high-confidence matches, we selected two distinct, highly interpretable machine learning classifiers:

  • Logistic Regression: Chosen because it acts as a linear classifier that outputs exact probabilities. It provides clear, highly interpretable feature coefficients, indicating which spatio-temporal variables carry the most weight.
  • Decision Trees: Selected because they represent a non-linear, rule-based classifier that handles feature interactions natively. They map out distinct, clear decision branches that ship route managers can trace directly to audit classification logic.
